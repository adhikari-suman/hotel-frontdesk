Lift Car

The front desk as a 1930s hotel lift. Occupancy reads from a brass floor-indicator dial, the 64 rooms stand as four landings of sixteen stepped deco doors, top floor first, and navigation is the floor indicator itself: a semicircular brass dial across the top of every screen where each screen is a numbered floor and the needle points to where you are. The admin screens are the “office floors”, past a key-switch segment on the arc: a receptionist sees them locked, an admin sees the key turned.

Rules the screens follow

The dial is the map

Navigation lives only on the floor-indicator arc. Desk floors 1 to 6 rise on the left, the key switch sits past them, office floors 7 to 9 descend on the right. The needle and the lit mint floor are where you are.

Doors carry three signals

Every room shows colour, a glyph (dot, arrow, ring, dashed ring, cross) and a word. Colour is never the only cue.

One brass-edged button

Each screen has one primary action: solid jade with a brass edge. Destructive actions are vermilion outlines and state the cost, e.g. “Cancel booking and charge $226.58”.
